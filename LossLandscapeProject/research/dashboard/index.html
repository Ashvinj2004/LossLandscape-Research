Flat in the Wrong Coordinates

Optimizer-induced symmetry drift biases sharpness comparisons between Adam and SGD. This page summarizes the experiments in the paper (paper/main.pdf) with the same figures.

Summary

A ReLU network computes the same function if one neuron's incoming weights are multiplied by some c > 0 and its outgoing weights divided by c. Most sharpness measures (Hessian trace, random weight noise, SAM sharpness) are not invariant to this, so they mix two things: how sharp the learned function is, and where on this set of equivalent weights the network sits. SGD cannot move between equivalent weights to first order. Adam can, and it drifts toward the weights where its trace is close to the minimum. On a CIFAR-10 MLP this alone makes Adam look about 2× flatter, which is enough to reverse the ranking against SGD.

Definitions

Rescaling symmetry
Multiply a hidden neuron's incoming weights and bias by c > 0 and divide its outgoing weights by c. The output does not change. All such rescalings of a network form its orbit.
Conserved quantity Q
Q = ‖w_in‖² − ‖w_out‖² per neuron. Gradient flow conserves it (Du et al. 2018).
Minimum sharpness
The smallest Hessian trace over the orbit (Ibayashi et al. 2021). Depends only on the function; computed exactly from the curvature diagonal.
Orbit excess
Raw trace / minimum sharpness (≥ 1). So raw trace = minimum sharpness × orbit excess, a function part times a coordinate part.
Loss-matched comparison
Each run is measured when its training loss first drops below 1, 0.3, 0.1, 0.03 and 0.01, and optimizers are compared at equal loss rather than equal epochs.

Mechanism: Adam drifts, SGD does not

90 runs
  • Each step changes Q by a first-order term plus a second-order term. For SGD the first-order term is zero by Euler's identity (measured below 3×10⁻⁷). For Adam it is 0.02–6.2, 23–800× its second-order term.
  • Adam's drift scales as η0.91 (√fan-in)0.94 at small learning rates, close to the sign-descent prediction η√fan-in.
  • RMSprop and signSGD behave the same way: excess 1.10–1.28, against 1.68–2.41 for SGD and SGD+momentum.
  • CIFAR-10 networks start at excess 3.66; Adam goes to about 1.2 and stays there.

The ranking depends on the measure

144 runs · 4 optimizers · 3 seeds
  • CIFAR-10: the relative-loss noise metric, isotropic noise and the Hessian trace rate Adam flatter (P ≈ 0.62–0.75). Minimum sharpness and multiplicative noise rate it sharper (P ≈ 0.01–0.31). Filter-normalized noise also rates it sharper, but it is only invariant to row scaling.
  • The coordinate part is about −1 bit at every loss level, larger than the function part (+0.4 to +0.7 bits).
  • MNIST: the coordinate part changes sign during training, so the raw ranking flips. The function part never favours Adam.
  • SAM comes out clearly flatter than Adam only under invariant measures.

Intervention: removing the drift

Adam-Q · 36 runs

Adam-Q is Adam followed, after every update, by a rescaling of each neuron that restores Q to its initial value. The function is unchanged.

  • CIFAR-10: orbit excess 1.18 → 2.35; raw trace ×1.91, isotropic noise ×1.96, relative-loss metric ×1.64; minimum sharpness ×0.98. Test accuracy unchanged.
  • MNIST: the excess barely changes (1.21 → 1.17), and neither do the raw measures.
  • Against SGD, Adam-Q is no longer rated flatter (P 0.64–0.75 → 0.21–0.35).
Open questionIn 6 of 18 CIFAR-10 configurations (the noisiest ones) Adam-Q stalled at loss 0.05–0.3 while Adam reached 0.01, so the drift may also help Adam train.

Sharpness and generalization

Kendall τ with the generalization gap
  • Within one optimizer, raw trace and minimum sharpness predict the gap about equally well (τ ≈ 0.55 vs 0.59).
  • Across optimizers the raw trace drops to 0.38 (CIFAR-10) and 0.47 (MNIST); minimum sharpness stays at 0.61 and 0.62.
  • The relative-loss noise metric is the weakest cross-optimizer predictor (τ = 0.36 and 0.14).

Replication of the original experiment

5 seeds · 20 runs

The first version of this project (the TensorFlow scripts in LossLandscapeProject/) found Adam flatter than SGD after 20 epochs. Re-running that protocol (raw 0–1 inputs, full datasets, 20 epochs, batch 64, SGD lr 0.01, Adam lr 0.001) with five seeds reproduces the result, for a different reason on each dataset.

Convolutional network

18 runs

Two conv layers and two dense layers on CIFAR-10, with the rescaling applied per channel and carried through max-pooling and flatten.

  • Adam's orbit excess stays at 1.45–1.78; SGD and SGD+M climb to about 3.2.
  • The coordinate part favours Adam by −0.31 → −0.93 bits against SGD over training; all CIs exclude zero.
  • Here Adam is much sharper at the function level (+0.7 to +2.3 bits), so the bias hides the difference rather than reversing it. Against SGD+M the raw trace shows no clear difference (P = 0.25–0.50) while every invariant measure rates Adam about 2× sharper (P = 0.00).

The three Adam runs at the lower learning rate hit the 150-epoch cap at loss 0.018–0.034, so the loss-0.01 comparison uses the other three.

Long runs: a fast and a slow phase

32 runs · 400–600 epochs

Implicit-regularization theory predicts that with persistent gradient noise SGD drifts toward minimum trace (Blanc et al.; Damian et al.) and Adam toward minimum Σ√Gii (Li et al. 2025). On the rescaling orbit both targets can be computed exactly.

  • Fast phase: on CIFAR-10 Adam goes from excess 3.7 to 1.09–1.13 within 5 epochs; SGD is at 1.45–1.63.
  • Without label noise SGD's coordinates stop moving once the data are fit.
  • With label noise SGD's trace excess keeps falling (CIFAR-10 1.63 → 1.24 at η = 0.12), and Adam's Σ√G excess falls (1.99 → 1.64) while its trace excess rises.
  • Per run: trace excess falls more than Σ√G excess in 8/8 SGD runs, and the reverse in 8/8 Adam runs (sign test p = 0.008 each).

Teleporting SGD to Adam's coordinates

negative result · 32 runs

Setup

Every 100 steps, SGD or SGD+M is moved to the minimum-trace point of its orbit (function unchanged, momentum buffer transformed accordingly). Four learning rates per optimizer, two seeds, CIFAR-10 MLP.

Result

10 of 16 teleported runs diverged, against 0 of 16 without teleports. Each teleport raised λmax by 3–20%, to within 5% of or above 2/η in 8 of 11 cases. Since SGD already trains near the edge of stability, the teleports push it over.

Starting point on the orbit

48 runs

Since SGD conserves Q, one might expect it to keep the excess it starts with. It does not.

  • Both optimizers lose the initial differences within one epoch: a 7× (MNIST) or 17× (CIFAR-10) spread shrinks to 1.3–1.9× and ends at 1.03–1.19×.
  • In weight space SGD keeps 90–99% of the separation in Q (79–83% with label noise); Adam widens it to 1.5–2.0× in 7 of 8 runs.
  • Adam ends below SGD from every start: 1.29–1.51 vs 1.71–1.89 (MNIST), 1.09–1.35 vs 1.78–2.04 (CIFAR-10).
  • With label noise the slow-phase result replicates: 12/12 runs per optimizer (p = 0.0005 each), 20/20 over both experiments.
PartialPer neuron, Adam's drift matches the Σ√G balance better than the trace balance in all 24 runs. SGD matches the trace balance on MNIST (6/6) but not on CIFAR-10 (0/6), although the network as a whole still moves toward minimum trace.

Depth

36 runs
  • The coordinate part does not grow with depth: −0.48 to −0.74 bits against SGD at every depth.
  • The function part does: against SGD, from +0.26–0.37 bits (1 layer) to +0.76–0.89 bits (8 layers).
  • At 1–2 layers the raw trace rates Adam flatter in every pair and the invariant measure rates it sharper in every pair. At 8 layers the bias hides most of the difference instead.

BatchNorm and weight decay

24 runs

With BatchNorm, scaling a row of pre-BN weights changes nothing, and curvature scales like 1/(row norm)². The norm is mostly set by the learning rate.

  • Raw measures follow the norm (Spearman −0.62 to −0.84). Pooled, they rate Adam sharper (P = 0.25), but the result depends on which learning rates are paired: 0/9 to 9/9.
  • Invariant measures (Σw²Hii, ASAM, multiplicative) do not follow the norm and rate Adam flatter in every pairing (70/72 and 72/72 pairs).
  • Weight decay at these strengths has little effect before loss 0.1 at matched learning rate.
DirectionInvariant measures rate Adam sharper on the plain MLP, the CNN and ResNet-18, but flatter in this BatchNorm MLP, so the paper makes no general claim about which optimizer is flatter.

ResNet-18 on full CIFAR-10

24 GPU runs

ResNet-18 has BatchNorm after every convolution, so this tests the BatchNorm regime, not the ReLU-orbit decomposition.

  • Without augmentation the raw trace follows the filter norms (Spearman −0.62 to −0.96), and its verdict is all or nothing per learning-rate pairing: Adam flatter in 22 of 54 comparisons.
  • Σw²Hii and ASAM rate Adam sharper in 53 and 54 of 54, about 2.1–2.9× against SGD. This is the opposite direction from the BatchNorm MLP.
  • With the standard recipe (93–95% test accuracy) the raw trace rates AdamW 1.3–4.9× flatter than SGD+M in every seed, mostly through its larger filters; the invariant measures do not confirm it.
  • Adam with coupled L2 weight decay collapses many deep filters to zero and is left out of the comparison.
